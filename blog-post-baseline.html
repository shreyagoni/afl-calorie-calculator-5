Your calorie target is a
starting point.

A daily estimate can give you useful context — as long as you leave room for real life, too.

Calory editorial team08 May 2024
≈
01

A calorie target is not a grade you receive at the end of the day. It is a working estimate — a place to begin noticing patterns and making small, informed adjustments.

Your body uses energy for more than workouts. Breathing, digestion, walking to the kitchen, concentrating through a meeting: it all adds up. That is why the most useful number is rarely the most exact-looking one.

Use the number as a lens

Try holding your target lightly for a couple of weeks. Notice your energy, hunger, sleep, and performance. A number that looks right on paper may need context from the person living it.

Good data should make your decisions feel clearer — not make your world feel smaller.

Leave room for the everyday

Some days are more active. Some meals are celebratory. Some mornings call for a slower start. A steady baseline can coexist with all of that. The goal is not perfect consistency; it is a relationship with food that feels sustainable.
